# Telco Customer Churn — Executive Summary

## Problem

Customer churn reduces recurring revenue and increases the effort needed to replace departing customers. This project uses the Telco Customer Churn dataset to identify patterns associated with churn, compare predictive models, and estimate whether targeted retention offers could be financially worthwhile. The dataset contains **7,043 customers** and **21 columns**.

## Three key findings

1. **Churn is material:** **1,869 of 7,043 customers churned (26.5%)**. Average monthly charges are **64.76** per customer, and total monthly charges across the dataset are **456,116.60**. These charge totals indicate exposure, not guaranteed revenue loss.

2. **The models identify many churners, but neither model is perfect:** at a **0.35 decision threshold**, logistic regression achieved **ROC-AUC 0.841**, **recall 90.6%**, and **precision 44.8%**. The HistGradientBoosting model achieved **ROC-AUC 0.838**, **recall 86.4%**, and **precision 46.7%**. Logistic regression is the slightly stronger overall ranking model and catches more churners at this threshold; its lower precision means many flagged customers will not churn.

3. **A targeted offer may be worth testing under the stated assumptions:** the top **10% of the held-out test set (141 customers)** had an average predicted churn probability of **90.41%** and average monthly charge of **79.05**. Assuming a **15% discount for 6 months** and **12 months** of remaining customer value, the estimated break-even intervention success rate is **8.30%**. Estimated net benefit is **14,152.21** at 20% success, **26,243.91** at 30%, and **38,335.60** at 40%. These are scenario outputs, not observed savings.

## Recommendation

Pilot a retention campaign with a small, high-risk segment and a clearly defined offer. Use the model to prioritize outreach, but compare contacted customers with a suitable control group to measure incremental retention. Track offer uptake, actual churn, discount cost, and retained revenue. Revisit the targeting threshold based on team capacity and the relative costs of missed churners versus unnecessary offers.

## Limitations

- Results describe the supplied dataset and may not generalize to another time period, market, or telecom provider.
- Predictive associations do not establish that a feature causes churn.
- At the selected threshold, precision is below 50%, so many flagged customers may stay without an offer.
- Revenue estimates depend on assumed offer success, discount, and remaining lifetime; they are not causal estimates and may overstate impact if customers would have stayed anyway.
- The dataset does not specify a currency in the project files; charge values are therefore reported in dataset units.
- The top-10% result is based on the held-out test split used by the revenue script. Dashboard scores generated by fitting on the full dataset are in-sample prototype scores and should not be used for production decisions. Calibrate probabilities and validate performance on later-period data before deployment.

---

**Decision:** Treat the model as a prioritization aid and validate the offer through a controlled pilot before scaling.
